## Naming Strategy Overview

### Market Context

The trading card marketplace space is dominated by:
- **TCGPlayer** (direct/descriptive, acquired by eBay for $295M)
- **Whatnot** (abstract/playful, live auction focus)
- **COMC** (Check Out My Cards -- acronym, purely functional)
- **Alt** (short, abstract, alternative assets angle)
- **Goldin** (surname-based, high-end auction)
- **StockX** (stock market metaphor, sneaker-first but expanding)
- **Cardmarket** (EU-focused, direct/descriptive)

**White space opportunity:** Most competitors either sound generic (TCGPlayer, Cardmarket) or completely disconnected from cards (Whatnot, Alt). There is room for a name that is evocative, premium-feeling, and clearly signals "cards" or "collecting" without being literal.

### Brand Pillars for Naming

| Pillar | What it means for the name |
|--------|---------------------------|
| AI/3D visualization | Should feel modern, not retro |
| Flat 3-5% fee | Trust, transparency, fairness |
| Community-first | Social, gathering, belonging |
| Premium but accessible | Not elitist, not cheap |
| Escrow/trust | Safety, reliability |

### 1. TROVE

**Category:** Metaphorical
**Pronunciation:** Rhymes with "stove"

#### Tagline Options
- "Your collection starts here."
- "Every card has a story. Find yours."
- "The marketplace collectors trust."

#### Why It Works
- **Meaning:** A trove is literally "a store of valuable or delightful things." That is exactly what a trading card marketplace is.
- **Memorability:** One syllable, five letters, universally understood. Easy to spell, easy to say.
- **Emotional resonance:** Evokes treasure hunting, discovery, hidden gems. Aligns perfectly with the thrill of finding a card you have been searching for.
- **Premium but not elitist:** A trove can be a pirate's chest or a grandmother's attic. Welcoming at every level.
- **Scalable:** Works beyond cards. Trove of comics, memorabilia, collectibles, anything.

#### Potential Concerns
- "Trove" is used by Trove (Australian newspaper aggregator by News Corp) and Trove (a defunct Trion Worlds MMO game). Neither is in the trading card or marketplace space, so direct conflict is low.
- Domain: trove.com is taken (likely parked or in use). trove.io, trovemarket.com, trovecard.com, gettrove.com are alternatives worth checking.
- Common English word, so trademark specificity depends on the class (marketplace/technology).

#### Logo Concept
An open treasure chest icon in a minimal, geometric style. The chest lid forms a subtle "T" shape. Inside the chest, a single card floats with a faint glow (representing the 3D/AI visualization). Color palette: deep navy or charcoal with gold accents. The wordmark uses a clean sans-serif with slightly rounded terminals for approachability.

An upward-angled arrow or chevron that doubles as a stylized "R." The arrow suggests upward movement (value going up). Bold, energetic color palette: electric blue or vibrant orange on white. The wordmark is in a geometric sans-serif with slightly italic lean (suggesting forward motion).

### 5. SLAB

**Category:** Action/Energy (Insider Term)
**Pronunciation:** One syllable, rhymes with "grab"

#### Tagline Options
- "Get your slab."
- "Graded cards. Flat fees. Fair deals."
- "The marketplace for serious cards."

#### Why It Works
- **Insider credibility:** In the card collecting world, a "slab" is a graded card encased in a hard plastic holder (PSA, BGS, CGC). Using this term signals immediately that the platform is built by collectors, for collectors.
- **Ultra-short:** Four letters, one syllable. Maximum punch. Works great as an app name.
- **Sounds solid:** The word "slab" has a physical, trustworthy weight to it. Fits the escrow/security angle.
- **Premium connotation:** Slabbed cards are the premium tier of the hobby. The name positions the platform at the top.
- **Verb-ready:** "I slabbed it" already exists in collector vocabulary (meaning: got it graded). Could extend to "I Slab'd that deal."

#### Potential Concerns
- **Exclusionary risk:** Casual collectors or newcomers may not know what "slab" means. Could feel like a gatekeeper term. Mitigated by tagline and onboarding.
- **Narrow association:** Strongly associated with graded cards specifically. If the platform also handles raw (ungraded) cards, the name could feel limiting.
- **Phonetics:** "Slab" can sound harsh or industrial. Needs warm, inviting brand design to soften it.
- Domain slab.com is almost certainly taken.

#### Logo Concept
A card inside a slab case, rendered in clean vector style with a slight 3D perspective (showing the depth of the case). A subtle holographic gradient on the slab itself hints at the AI/3D visualization. Bold, uppercase wordmark in a tight grotesque font. Color palette: PSA-red or BGS-black with white -- leveraging familiar grading company aesthetics but with a modern twist.

### 7. NEARMINT

**Category:** Insider Term / Descriptive
**Pronunciation:** NEER-mint (two words: Near Mint)

#### Tagline Options
- "Closer to perfect."
- "The marketplace that gets the details right."
- "Where condition meets community."

#### Why It Works
- **Collector language:** "Near Mint" (NM) is the most desirable and commonly traded card condition grade. Every collector knows this term. It signals insider knowledge and credibility.
- **Aspirational but realistic:** Near Mint is excellent but not impossibly perfect. This mirrors the platform's positioning: premium but accessible. Not just for whales.
- **Built-in quality signal:** The name implies high standards, careful handling, attention to condition.
- **Two-word compound:** Works well as "NearMint" (one word), "Near Mint" (two words), or "NM" (abbreviation for marketing).
- **Memorability:** Extremely high. If you know cards, you instantly get it. If you do not, it still sounds positive and clean.

#### Potential Concerns
- **Insider barrier:** Non-collectors will not immediately understand the grading reference. However, the words "near" and "mint" are both positive in everyday English, so it still works.
- **Grading specificity:** Could inadvertently signal that the platform is only for graded or high-condition cards, when it should serve all conditions.
- **Length:** Two words / eight letters is manageable but not ultra-short.

#### Logo Concept
A clean, modern wordmark where the "NM" abbreviation is prominent (as a badge/icon version). The full name uses a crisp sans-serif. A subtle holographic sheen or foil effect on the "Mint" portion nods to the premium card world. Could include a small diamond or gem icon (quality symbol). Color palette: mint green (obvious but effective) paired with charcoal or navy. Alternative: white and silver (clean, premium).

## Fee-Angle Names

Names that play on the platform's competitive 3-5% flat fee (vs. industry standard 8%+).

### 1. **FlatRate Cards** / **FlatRate**
- Directly communicates the value proposition.
- "FlatRate: 3%. That's it."
- Simple, honest, transparent. But generic and not very exciting.

### 2. **Three Percent** / **ThreePCT**
- Bold and specific. Turns the fee into the brand.
- "Welcome to Three Percent. The marketplace that keeps more money in your pocket."
- Risk: Locks you into a specific fee forever. Hard to adjust pricing.

### 3. **LessTax** / **LowTake**
- Positions against the competition's high take rates.
- "LowTake: More for sellers. More for buyers."
- Negative framing (defined by what you are not). Better as a campaign than a brand name.

### 4. **ClearFee**
- Transparency as the brand.
- "ClearFee: You see exactly what you pay."
- Clean but clinical. Better as a feature name than a brand.

### 5. **FairSwap**
- Fairness + trading action.
- "FairSwap: Where the deal is always fair."
- Accessible and values-driven. Could feel too soft or paternalistic.

**Recommendation:** The fee advantage is better used as a tagline or campaign rather than baked into the brand name. Names built on pricing are fragile -- they break when pricing changes and they define you by a number rather than an identity.

## Naming Patterns to Avoid

| Pattern | Why to Avoid | Examples |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| "Card" + generic noun | Forgettable, hard to trademark, SEO crowded | CardZone, CardStop, CardBase |
| Misspellings for domain | Looks unprofessional, hard to spell verbally | Kardz, Tradez, Kollect |
| Overly long compounds | Hard to type, hard to remember, bad as app name | TradingCardMarketplace, CollectorConnection |
| Crypto/web3 jargon | Alienates mainstream collectors, dated | CardDAO, MintChain, TokenDeck |
| Numbers in name | Feels like a sequel, hard to own | Card3D, Trade360 |
| Purely generic terms | Cannot trademark, SEO impossible | "Cards," "The Market," "Trade" |
